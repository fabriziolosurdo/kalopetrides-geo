Moving to Cyprus from the UK: 2026 Visas & Residency Guide

Moving to Cyprus from the UK after Brexit: which residence permit fits, the documents to prepare before you leave, GESY and S1 healthcare, UK pensions, driving and what the move costs.

Written by Sergios Charalambous, Partner

Cyprus Bar Association · Updated on 25 September 2026

Share

//Quick Answer

Can British citizens move to Cyprus after Brexit?

Yes. British citizens can visit Cyprus without a visa for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. To live there longer, a new arrival needs a residence permit such as the Pink Slip, a Digital Nomad permit, an employment permit, Category F or Regulation 6(2). UK nationals resident before 1 January 2021 may keep Withdrawal Agreement rights.

Moving to Cyprus from the UK is still very possible after Brexit, but it is no longer automatic. A British passport now gives you up to 90 days in any 180-day period as a visitor. To live in Cyprus beyond that, you need a residence permit that matches why you are moving: retirement or living on your own means, remote work, a Cyprus job, investment or family ties. British nationals who were already living in Cyprus before 1 January 2021 are the exception, because the Withdrawal Agreement may protect the rights they already have.

This guide covers the practical side of the move: which permit fits, the documents to prepare while you are still in the UK, healthcare, pensions, driving and the order to do things in. For the detailed UK Statutory Residence Test, split-year, treaty and temporary non-residence analysis, read our UK-to-Cyprus tax-residency and structuring guide.

The correct order for a UK-to-Cyprus move

First model the UK Statutory Residence Test for the departure year, including whether a specific split-year case is available. Second, select the Cyprus immigration route: Withdrawal Agreement status where already protected, EU-family rules where applicable, or the relevant third-country route. Third, establish Cyprus tax residence from actual days and conditions. Fourth, classify every income stream under domestic law and the UK–Cyprus treaty.

Review UK property, companies, director duties, pensions, ISAs and other wrappers, trusts, temporary non-residence and estate planning. UK tax wrappers do not necessarily preserve their treatment in Cyprus. A UK will and Cyprus will may be useful for separate asset pools, but their drafting must be coordinated with Regulation (EU) 650/2012, domicile, matrimonial property and the applicable succession law.

Primary checks: HMRC Statutory Residence Test guidance, HMRC foreign income guidance, Cyprus Migration Department instructions and the UK–Cyprus treaty.

UK status and practical-relocation checklist

Withdrawal Agreement protection is fact-specific. A UK national or qualifying family member who exercised EU free-movement residence in Cyprus before the end of the transition period and continues to reside there may retain protected residence and work rights. Cyprus applies the declaratory system under Article 18(4): eligible UK nationals may use earlier EU residence documents as evidence or obtain the newer UKW/MUKW document, while third-country family members have separate document requirements. A new post-transition arrival does not acquire Withdrawal Agreement rights merely by holding a British passport.

UK tax departure remains separate. Apply the Statutory Residence Test for every tax year; split-year treatment is available only where a specified case and all its conditions are met. A later UK return can bring specified income or gains within the temporary non-residence rules, so disposals and distributions should be modelled before execution.

  • Identify the legal route for every family member: Withdrawal Agreement beneficiary, EU-family route or current third-country category.
  • Preserve evidence of lawful residence before 1 January 2021 and continuity thereafter; verify whether an MEU or UKW/MUKW document is appropriate.
  • Map UK homes, workdays, ties and travel against the SRT before fixing the departure date or assuming split-year treatment.
  • Review healthcare cover, driving-licence exchange, schools, banking/source-of-funds, tenancy or purchase due diligence, and work permission as separate tasks.
  • Test pensions, UK property, companies, trusts, ISAs and planned gains under UK law, Cyprus law and the treaty, including the consequences of a possible UK return.

Official sources: Cyprus Migration Department Withdrawal Agreement and UKW guidance, GOV.UK living in Cyprus, HMRC Statutory Residence Test guidance, temporary non-residence guidance and the UK–Cyprus Double Taxation Convention.

Cyprus-side reference. 2026: For the current Cyprus day tests, SDC/GHS boundaries and our advisory scope, use the Cyprus tax-residence and non-dom guide and tax-residence and non-dom service. This page remains focused on the departure-country, treaty and cross-border decisions that are specific to this route.

Which Cyprus residence permit fits your move

Your reason for moving decides the permit, and the permit decides whether you may work. Choose it first: the documents, the income evidence and the timetable all follow from it.

If you also hold an Irish or another EU passport, you do not need any of these routes. You can register in Cyprus as an EU citizen with a Yellow Slip (form MEU1), and a British spouse or partner can then apply as your family member.

Retiring or living on your own means: the Pink Slip

Most British retirees apply for the visitor temporary residence permit, known as the Pink Slip. Each applicant files form MVIS8. The first permit is normally issued for one year and can be renewed while you keep meeting the conditions. The official fee is EUR 70, plus a further EUR 70 for first registration in the Aliens Register where that is required. The Migration Department says it usually examines a complete, well-documented file within four months.

Two conditions catch people out. The permit does not allow any economic activity in Cyprus, and that includes remote work for a UK employer. It is also cancelled if you leave Cyprus and do not return within three months, whatever date is printed on the card.

You will see a figure of about EUR 24,000 a year quoted online. It is not a universal official threshold. The Migration Department assesses the source, regularity and sufficiency of your income for your household under the current checklist. Pension statements and a steady record of transfers from abroad are the strongest evidence. Our Pink Slip lawyers prepare and file the application; the Pink Slip guide covers every document.

Working remotely for a UK employer or clients: the Digital Nomad permit

If you intend to keep working for a UK employer or UK clients, the Digital Nomad permit is designed for you. You need net monthly income of at least EUR 3,500 from employment or clients outside Cyprus. The scheme is capped at 500 permits, and the Migration Department targets five to seven weeks to examine a complete application. The permit settles your immigration status only; where you are tax resident is a separate test.

Settling permanently: Category F or Regulation 6(2)

Category F is a permanent immigration permit for people with secure income from abroad who will not work in Cyprus. Regulation 6(2) permanent residence is the expedited investor route, with an estimated examination period of about two months for a complete file. Neither permit makes you tax resident or non-domiciled, and neither creates Cypriot citizenship on its own.

Documents to prepare while you are still in the UK

Several documents are far easier to obtain before you leave. UK documents are already in English, which saves translation, but most still need an apostille.

  • ACRO police certificate. Your UK criminal record certificate must come from the ACRO Criminal Records Office, not a DBS check. It must be no older than six months when you submit the application, and apostilled.
  • Apostilled civil documents. Marriage or civil-partnership certificates and children's birth certificates prove family relationships. The FCDO Legalisation Office issues UK apostilles.
  • A passport with enough validity. A permit cannot outlast the passport: a Pink Slip ends three months before the passport expires. Renew first if yours runs out within the coming year.
  • Evidence of income and its source. Pension award letters, employment or client contracts and bank statements showing regular income from abroad.
  • Accommodation in Cyprus. A long-term rental agreement or evidence of the property you are buying.
  • Health insurance. Valid private medical cover is required for the visitor application.

The medical evidence, the exact forms and the accepted clinics change from time to time. Follow the current Migration Department checklist for your category on the filing date rather than an older application pack.

Healthcare: GESY, S1 and private insurance

Cyprus has a national health system, GESY, but a British national does not join it automatically on arrival.

  • New arrivals on a visitor permit rely on private health insurance, which is also a condition of the permit.
  • UK state pensioners with an S1 form issued by the UK can register with GESY once they hold a Cyprus residence permit and own or rent a home in Cyprus. A Health Care Card from the Ministry of Health is needed before GESY registration.
  • People employed in Cyprus join GESY through their contributions.
  • Withdrawal Agreement residents have their own rules: a UKW1 holder can use GESY while employed in Cyprus, and one who is not employed needs permanent residence (UKW3).

Keep your private cover in place until your GESY registration is confirmed.

Retiring to Cyprus from the UK

Retiring to Cyprus from the UK is the most common reason British clients give us for the move. The usual immigration route is the Pink Slip; Category F suits those who want permanent status from the start. The real planning is about pensions, healthcare and succession.

  • Where your pension is taxed. Under the UK–Cyprus treaty, most UK private and state pensions paid to a Cyprus tax resident are taxable in Cyprus. Pensions for UK government service follow a separate article of the treaty and are generally taxed only in the UK.
  • The 5% option. A Cyprus tax resident can elect, year by year, to pay 5% on foreign pension income above EUR 5,000 instead of the normal scale rates. On a EUR 25,000 pension that is EUR 1,000 of tax. Our guide to UK pensions in Cyprus compares both options.
  • Stopping UK deduction at source. Your pension provider keeps deducting UK tax until HMRC authorises gross payment. The claim is made under the treaty using form DT-Individual.
  • Healthcare. Request your S1 form from the UK before you move if you receive the UK State Pension.
  • Wills. Assets in both countries are often easier to administer with coordinated wills. See why you may need two separate wills.

Tax residence is decided by your days in Cyprus and the 183-day or 60-day test, not by the permit. Plan the first calendar year before you book the move.

Driving, banking and daily life

Much of Cyprus will feel familiar. Traffic drives on the left, homes use UK-style three-pin plugs and English is widely spoken, although Greek is the official language and the currency is the euro.

  • Driving licence. Once Cyprus becomes your usual residence (the 185-day rule), you need to deal with your UK licence. Whether it can be exchanged directly depends on the Road Transport Department's current rules; our driving-licence guide sets out the TOM 7D process.
  • Banking. Cyprus banks apply full due diligence to new residents. Bring evidence of your income and the source of your funds, and expect questions about UK accounts you keep. See opening a bank account in Cyprus.
  • Working locally. A Cyprus job needs an employment permit for that employer and role unless you hold Withdrawal Agreement or EU status.

What the move costs

There is no single official figure for how much money you need to move to Cyprus. Each route has its own test:

  • Visitor permit (Pink Slip): stable resources assessed for your household. The official fees are EUR 70 for the permit and EUR 70 for first registration where needed.
  • Digital Nomad permit: at least EUR 3,500 net a month, plus 20% for a partner and 15% for each child.
  • Category F: a published baseline of EUR 9,568.17 a year, plus EUR 4,613.22 for each dependant.
  • Regulation 6(2): a qualifying investment of at least EUR 300,000 and EUR 50,000 secured annual income.

On top of those, budget for the ACRO certificate and apostilles in the UK, private medical insurance, the medical tests, a rental deposit and removals. Official sources: Cyprus Migration Department pages for visitors and family members, investor permanent residence and employment permits.

This guide sets out the usual routes and the decisions to take. It does not confirm that a permit will be granted, that UK residence has ended or that tax has been reduced. Take coordinated advice for both countries before changing your home, pensions, payroll or company management.
