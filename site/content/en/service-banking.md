## How it works in Cyprus

Banking and finance work in Cyprus runs on two layers. The first is EU law: regulations that apply directly, such as the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation and the sanctions regulations, and directives that Cyprus has transposed into national statutes, such as the payment services and investment services laws. The second is Cyprus law proper: the Companies Law, Cap. 113, the Civil Procedure Law, Cap. 6, the Immovable Property (Transfer and Mortgage) Law 9/1965, and the anti-money-laundering law. Almost every matter touches both layers.

Three authorities matter most. The Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC) receives applications for banking licences and cooperates with the European Central Bank, which is exclusively competent to authorise credit institutions in the Single Supervisory Mechanism; the CBC itself licenses payment institutions and electronic money institutions. CySEC authorises investment firms, fund managers and funds, and is the competent authority for crypto-asset service providers. The Cyprus courts, applying the Civil Procedure Rules in force since 2023 for new cases, handle debt recovery and insolvency.

A typical engagement follows the same path regardless of the topic. The firm first establishes the facts: who the client is, where the money comes from, what the transaction is meant to achieve and which counterparties are involved. It then identifies the applicable regime, lists the documents that the bank, the regulator or the court will require, and prepares them in the form Cyprus law demands. Finally it manages the filing or the application and the questions that follow, so that the client is not left to interpret a regulator's letter on their own.

## Opening a bank account in Cyprus as a foreign client

Cyprus banks are obliged entities under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Law 188(I)/2007. The law requires them to identify every customer, to identify the natural person who ultimately owns or controls a corporate customer, and to understand the purpose of the relationship and the origin of the funds. Banks apply these duties through their own onboarding procedures, which differ from one institution to another and which change over time.

For an individual, this generally means a valid passport, proof of address, information about profession and income and, for larger balances, documentary evidence of where the money came from. For a company, the bank will want the corporate documents, the identity of every director and shareholder up to the ultimate beneficial owner, a description of the business, the expected volume and geography of payments, and often contracts or invoices that prove the activity is real. A company that cannot explain its ownership or its cash flow in plain terms will usually be declined.

Kalopetrides Law LLC does not promise that a bank will accept a client, because the decision is the bank's. What the firm does is prepare a complete and consistent file, choose the bank whose risk appetite matches the client's profile, answer the bank's questions in the language of its compliance department, and keep the account in good standing afterwards when the bank asks for periodic updates.

## Loan agreements and security

Cyprus law recognises the security instruments that an international lender expects. A mortgage over immovable property is created under the Immovable Property (Transfer and Mortgage) Law 9/1965: the law provides that no mortgage is valid unless it is made in accordance with its procedure, and that procedure runs through a declaration at the District Lands Office and registration of the mortgage against the title of the property. An unregistered private agreement does not bind the property. A pledge over shares in a Cyprus company, a fixed or floating charge over company assets, an assignment of receivables and personal or corporate guarantees are the other instruments in common use. Charges created by a Cyprus company are registered with the Registrar of Companies in accordance with the Companies Law, Cap. 113.

The firm drafts and negotiates facility agreements, term loans and intra-group loans; prepares the security documents and the corporate approvals that go with them; checks title and existing encumbrances at the Lands Office; and attends to registration. Where a transaction is governed by foreign law, the firm issues or reviews the Cyprus-law parts, such as the capacity of the Cyprus borrower or guarantor and the perfection of Cyprus security, and coordinates with the lender's counsel in the lender's jurisdiction.

## Debt recovery and enforcement

Recovering money in Cyprus starts with a formal demand and, if that fails, a claim before the competent District Court. Cyprus has also established a Commercial Court by Law 69(I)/2022 for commercial claims above a statutory value threshold; the firm confirms at the time of filing which court is competent for the claim. Once a judgment is obtained, enforcement is governed by the Civil Procedure Law, Cap. 6, which provides for seizure and sale of movable property, sale of immovable property by public auction, and attachment of property held by third parties, which is how a creditor reaches a debtor's bank balances.

A judgment obtained in another EU Member State in a civil or commercial matter is enforceable in Cyprus under Regulation (EU) No 1215/2012 (Brussels I recast). Judgments from outside the EU are enforced under the relevant bilateral or multilateral treaty where one exists, or under the general rules of Cyprus law.

The firm advises creditors on the realistic prospects of recovery before money is spent on proceedings, conducts searches on the debtor's assets, obtains interim measures where there is a risk that assets will be moved, and runs the enforcement process to its end. It also acts for debtors who receive a demand or a claim and need to negotiate, restructure or defend.

## Insolvency and restructuring

Corporate insolvency in Cyprus is governed by the Companies Law, Cap. 113. The main procedures are winding up by the court, voluntary winding up by members or creditors, and receivership. In 2015 Cyprus added examinership to the Companies Law through a Part on the appointment of an examiner: a company in financial difficulty, or a creditor, may apply to the court for the appointment of an examiner to assess a rescue plan, and the company's assets are protected from individual enforcement while the procedure runs. A Department of Insolvency now administers insolvency matters that were previously handled by the Official Receiver.

For a creditor, the choice between enforcement and a winding-up petition depends on whether the debtor has assets worth pursuing and whether other creditors are already in the queue. For a company in difficulty, the question is whether a restructuring can be agreed with lenders privately, whether a formal procedure is needed to bind dissenting creditors, and how directors can discharge their duties while the company is insolvent. Kalopetrides Law LLC advises on all of these, represents creditors and companies in insolvency proceedings and deals with liquidators and examiners on the client's behalf.

## Regulatory licensing with CySEC and the Central Bank of Cyprus

Cyprus licenses most types of regulated financial business, and the licence is passportable across the European Economic Area under the corresponding EU directive. The regime depends on the activity:

- **Investment firms (CIFs).** Authorised by CySEC under the Investment Services and Activities and Regulated Markets Law 87(I)/2017, which transposes MiFID II. The application covers the shareholders and directors, the organisational structure, capital, internal controls and the services the firm will provide.
- **Funds and fund managers.** Alternative investment funds and their managers are regulated under the Alternative Investment Funds Law 124(I)/2018 and supervised by CySEC.
- **Payment institutions and electronic money institutions.** Licensed by the Central Bank of Cyprus under the Payment Services and Access to Payment Systems Law 31(I)/2018 and the Electronic Money Law 81(I)/2012. The CBC's published licensing procedure provides for a pre-application exploratory stage before the formal application is submitted and assessed.
- **Credit institutions.** The CBC receives the application and works with the European Central Bank, which grants the licence.

The firm prepares the application file, drafts the policies and manuals that regulators expect, liaises with the regulator during the assessment and advises licensed entities on ongoing compliance, including the periodic reporting and the anti-money-laundering obligations that apply to them under Law 188(I)/2007.

## Fintech and crypto-asset service providers

Crypto-asset services in the EU are governed by Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 on markets in crypto-assets (MiCA). Before MiCA applied in full, Cyprus registered crypto-asset service providers under its anti-money-laundering law, in a register kept by CySEC. Article 143(3) of MiCA allowed providers already operating under national law to continue during a transitional period, and Cyprus used the full period of eighteen months, ending on 1 July 2026 or on the date a MiCA application was granted or refused, whichever came first.

Since 1 July 2026, therefore, a provider of crypto-asset services in or from Cyprus needs a MiCA authorisation from CySEC. A national registration is no longer sufficient on its own. The Central Bank of Cyprus is involved where a credit institution or an electronic money institution issues e-money tokens or notifies its intention to provide crypto-asset services.

Kalopetrides Law LLC advises start-ups and established fintech businesses on which regime their model falls under, prepares MiCA authorisation applications and the related governance and anti-money-laundering documentation, and advises providers that have not obtained authorisation on how to proceed lawfully.

## Sanctions and compliance for clients from Russia and Ukraine

EU sanctions regulations apply directly in Cyprus as in every other Member State. The most relevant for clients with links to Russia or Ukraine is Council Regulation (EU) No 833/2014, as amended, together with the asset-freeze regulations that list specific persons and entities. Cyprus banks screen clients and transactions against these lists, and Cyprus established a National Sanctions Implementation Unit within the Ministry of Finance by Law 150(I)/2025 to coordinate enforcement and to process licence and derogation requests.

In practice, a client with a Russian or Ukrainian connection will face more questions from a Cyprus bank, not fewer: on citizenship and residence, on the ownership of the companies involved, on the origin of funds and on the counterparties to each payment. Much of what is lawful is still difficult to execute without the right documentation. The firm does not offer ways around the rules. It advises whether a given person, entity or transaction is affected, prepares the documentation a bank or regulator will expect, applies for a licence or derogation where the regulation provides for one, and tells the client clearly when something cannot be done.
