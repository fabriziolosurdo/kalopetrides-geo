  - q: What is the minimum investment for permanent residence in Cyprus?
    a: Under the Immigration Permit policy based on Regulation 6(2) of the Aliens and Immigration Regulations, the investment is at least €300,000 in one of four categories — a new house or apartment bought from a developer (plus VAT), other real estate such as offices, shops or hotels, the share capital of a Cyprus company that employs at least five people, or units of a Cyprus collective investment organisation. The applicant must also show a secure annual income of at least €50,000, increased for a spouse and each dependent child. The revised policy has applied since 2 May 2023.
  - q: Can I work in Cyprus with an investor permanent residence permit?
    a: No, with one exception. The applicant and spouse must confirm that they will not take up employment in Cyprus. They may act as directors of the company they invested in, and may hold shares in Cyprus companies and serve as unpaid directors; dividends from those holdings do not prevent approval. If you intend to run a business and be paid for it, a work permit through a registered foreign-interest company is usually the better route.
  - q: Do EU citizens need a residence permit in Cyprus?
    a: EU, EEA and Swiss citizens do not need a permit, but they must register. Anyone staying longer than three months applies for a Registration Certificate (form MEU1) within four months of arrival, showing employment, self-employment, or sufficient resources plus sickness insurance. The certificate does not expire. After five years of uninterrupted legal residence, an EU citizen can apply for a Permanent Residence Certificate (form MEU3). Failing to register can lead to a fine.
  - q: Is the Cyprus Digital Nomad Visa still available?
    a: The scheme exists and is run by the Civil Registry and Migration Department, but it has a cap on the number of permits — raised by the Council of Ministers to 500 in March 2022 — so availability can change. Whether applications are being accepted on a given day has to be checked with the Department before you plan around it. The income requirement is a net monthly income of at least €3,500 from work performed remotely for employers or clients outside Cyprus.
  - q: How many years of residence do I need to apply for Cypriot citizenship?
    a: Under Law 149(I)/2023, which amended the Civil Registry Law, the general rule is legal and continuous residence for the twelve months immediately before the application (absences of up to 90 days in total do not count) plus at least seven years of legal residence in aggregate during the ten years before those twelve months. Knowledge of Greek at CEFR level B1 is required. Highly qualified employees of employers designated by the Council of Ministers benefit from a shorter residence period.
  - q: Can my family join me in Cyprus if I hold a work permit?
    a: Yes, through family reunification. As a general rule the sponsor must have resided legally in Cyprus for two years, hold a residence permit valid for at least one year and have a reasonable prospect of permanent residence; the two-year condition is waived for employees of companies approved to employ foreign personnel. The spouse must be at least 21 and the marriage at least one year old at the date of application. Minor unmarried children are eligible. Where a condition is not yet met, a dependent visitor permit may be available in the meantime.
  - q: What is long-term resident status and how is it different from permanent residence?
    a: Long-term resident status is an EU-based status for third-country nationals who have lived legally and continuously in the Republic for five years, with absences of less than six consecutive months and no more than ten months in total. The permit is issued for five years and is renewable. It is different from the investor Immigration Permit, which depends on maintaining an investment, and from the EU citizens' MEU3 certificate. Some categories — students, seasonal workers, asylum seekers — are excluded.

Kalopetrides Law LLC helps people and companies obtain, renew and keep the right to live and work in Cyprus. For a non-EU national that means choosing the correct permit — visitor, employment, investor permanent residence, digital nomad, family reunification, long-term residence or citizenship — and filing a complete, certified application with the Civil Registry and Migration Department. For an EU citizen the route is simpler: registration rather than a permit. In both cases the firm prepares the file, deals with the Department on your behalf and says plainly what the rules allow and what they do not.

## How it works in Cyprus

Immigration in the Republic of Cyprus is administered by the Civil Registry and Migration Department (CRMD) under the Aliens and Immigration Law and its Regulations, with separate rules for Union citizens under EU free-movement law. Almost every route follows the same sequence.

### 1. Choosing the route

The categories do not overlap well. A visitor permit allows no economic activity. An investor Immigration Permit does not allow employment. An employment permit is tied to one employer. Long-term residence and citizenship count only certain kinds of prior residence. Choosing the wrong one costs months.

### 2. Entry

Third-country nationals normally need an entry visa from a Cypriot consular authority, or an entry permit issued in Cyprus for employment and some other categories. Entry must be through the legal ports and airports of the Republic.

### 3. Registration, biometrics and the file

First-time applicants are registered in the Aliens' Register and give biometrics in person. Every document issued outside Cyprus must be officially translated and duly certified. Applications with missing documents are not accepted, so the firm issues a document list specific to your nationality and category before anything is sent.

### 4. After approval

Most permits carry continuing conditions — maintaining an investment, health insurance, renewal before expiry, absence limits. The firm tracks these dates for its clients.

## Permanent residence for investors (Regulation 6(2))

The Immigration Permit under Regulation 6(2) of the Aliens and Immigration Regulations is the route most foreign property buyers ask about. The current policy, in force since 2 May 2023, requires an investment of at least €300,000 in one of four categories:

- **(A)** a house or apartment bought as a first sale from a development company, worth at least €300,000 plus VAT;
- **(B)** other real estate — offices, shops, hotels or similar developments — totalling €300,000, resales permitted;
- **(C)** €300,000 in the share capital of a Cyprus company with physical presence in Cyprus that employs at least five people;
- **(D)** €300,000 in units of Cyprus collective investment organisations (AIF, AIFLNP, RAIF) whose investments are held in Cyprus.

The applicant must also show a secure annual income of at least €50,000, increased by €15,000 for a spouse and €10,000 for each dependent minor child. For option (A) the income must come from abroad; for (B), (C) and (D) part of it may come from activities in Cyprus.

The condition people miss: the applicant and spouse undertake not to take up employment in Cyprus, except as directors of the company they invested in. Dependent student children aged 18 to 25 can apply separately with an extra €10,000 of parental income each; other adult children require a further €300,000 of investment per child.

After approval the holder must take up residence in Cyprus within one year, provide annual evidence that the investment is maintained, hold health insurance if not covered by GESY, and renew criminal record certificates every three years. The permit lapses if the holder obtains permanent residence elsewhere or stays out of Cyprus for two years. The residence card is valid for ten years and must be renewed.

## Temporary residence as a visitor (the "pink slip")

A visitor's permit under Regulation 14 — informally the pink slip — is for third-country nationals who come for holidays, touring or to explore the possibility of living in Cyprus. The holder cannot carry out any economic activity in the Republic. The first permit is issued for one year and can be renewed while the criteria are still met. Categories include autonomous visitors, dependent spouses and minor children, and dependent partners of Cypriot citizens in a proven long-term relationship. It is a holding position, not a path in itself: time spent as a visitor counts differently for later statuses, and the firm will say so if a client's real aim is work, business or citizenship.

## Work permits and the Business Facilitation Unit

For most third-country nationals who want to work in Cyprus, the practical route runs through an employer registered with the Business Facilitation Unit (BFU) as a company of foreign interests. The current registration policy has applied since 12 December 2022; applications are online-only.

To register, a company must be majority-owned by third-country nationals (or have a foreign minority shareholder with a stake worth at least €200,000) and the ultimate beneficial owner must prove an initial investment of at least €200,000 in Cyprus, made no more than six months before the application. Shipping, high-tech, pharma/biotech and some other categories qualify subject to the same investment proof. The BFU completes registration within ten business days of a duly submitted e-form and notifies the CRMD.

Once registered, the company can employ third-country nationals under the Strategy for Attracting Businesses and Talent (revised June 2023): highly skilled staff at a minimum gross monthly salary of €2,500, with permits of up to three years; third-country nationals may make up to 70% of total staff over five years, and up to 30% of support staff. Spouses of employees earning at least €2,500 gross per month (other than support staff) have free access to the labour market. Where a family reunification condition such as the one-year marriage rule is not yet met, a dependent visitor permit is available, for which the sponsor shows an annual income of at least €24,000, increased by 20% for a spouse or partner and 15% for each child.

Employers carry obligations too — staff ratios, timely renewals, documented salaries — and Kalopetrides Law LLC handles this side as well as the employee's permit.

## Digital Nomad Visa

The Digital Nomad Visa is for non-EU/EEA nationals who work remotely for an employer registered abroad or as self-employed with clients abroad. The applicant must show a stable net monthly income of at least €3,500 after taxes and contributions (increased by 20% for a spouse or partner and 15% per child under the Ministry of Finance strategy). The first permit is valid for one year; renewals are for up to two years and must be requested at least one month before expiry. A spouse or civil partner and minor children can obtain their own permits for the same period but may not work.

Two cautions. The scheme has a ceiling of 500 permits (set by the Council of Ministers in March 2022) so availability must be checked with the Department before applying. And more than 183 days in Cyprus in a tax year makes you Cyprus tax resident under section 2 of the Income Tax Law; if your home country also treats you as resident, the double tax treaty decides.

## EU citizens: registration, not a permit

If you are a citizen of Germany, Austria, the Netherlands, Poland or any other EU, EEA or Swiss state, you do not apply for a residence permit. For stays of up to three months a valid passport or ID card is enough. For longer stays you apply within four months of entry for a Registration Certificate — form MEU1 — showing that you are employed or self-employed, or that you have sufficient resources and comprehensive sickness insurance. The certificate does not expire; not registering can bring a fine of up to €2,500. Third-country family members of an EU citizen apply for a Residence Card (MEU2).

After five years of uninterrupted legal residence, EU citizens apply for a Permanent Residence Certificate (MEU3); third-country family members obtain a ten-year Permanent Residence Card under the same form. For German and Austrian readers: there is no municipal Anmeldung — registration is done with the CRMD, and the certificate is what banks, tax authorities and employers will ask to see.

## Family reunification

Third-country nationals living in Cyprus can bring their spouse and minor unmarried children through family reunification under the Aliens and Immigration Law. The sponsor must, as a rule, have lived legally in Cyprus for at least two years, hold a residence permit valid for at least one year and have a reasonable prospect of permanent residence; the two-year condition is waived for employees of companies approved to employ foreign personnel. The spouse must be at least 21 and the marriage at least one year old when the application is filed. The sponsor files, and the family member does not travel on a tourist visa except where the Department allows it.

## Long-term resident status

A third-country national who has resided legally and continuously in the government-controlled areas for five years, holding a valid residence permit, can apply for long-term resident status. Absences do not break the period if each is shorter than six consecutive months and they total no more than ten months over the five years. The permit is issued for five years and is renewable. Students, asylum seekers, seasonal and other temporary workers are excluded. The status can be withdrawn after twelve consecutive months outside the EU or six years outside Cyprus. Applications are filed in person at the Department's Nicosia offices; decisions take six to twelve months.

## Citizenship by naturalisation

Law 149(I)/2023, published on 19 December 2023, rewrote the residence-based route to Cypriot citizenship in the Civil Registry Law. The general rule has two parts: legal and continuous residence for the twelve months immediately before the application, where absences totalling up to 90 days are disregarded, plus at least seven years of legal residence (or public service) in aggregate during the ten years before those twelve months. Applicants must show Greek at CEFR level B1 under the certificates set by ministerial decree, and the law provides for further integration conditions.

The same law created a fast track for highly qualified persons employed by employers designated by Council of Ministers decision — in practice the registered companies of foreign interests. For them the seven-year requirement falls to four years with Greek at level A2 or three years with level B1, and applications are to be decided within eight months. Spouses, civil partners and adult children of such employees may also apply.

Naturalisation is discretionary. The firm's role is to make sure the residence history is counted correctly — a frequent problem for clients whose earlier years were spent on visitor or student permits — and that the file is complete when lodged.

## Readers from Russia, Ukraine, Israel and other non-EU countries

Russian and Israeli citizens are third-country nationals and use the routes above. For Russian clients the legal routes have not changed, but the practical environment has: banks apply enhanced source-of-funds checks, transfers can be delayed or refused, and EU restrictive measures must be reviewed before any investment-based application. Kalopetrides Law LLC examines these points at the start and says clearly when a route is not realistic.

Ukrainian citizens who left Ukraine because of the war and entered Cyprus on or after 1 February 2022 may be covered by temporary protection under Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2022/382 — residence, access to the labour market, housing, medical care and schooling — applied for at the District Immigration Offices of the Police. The EU Council has extended it to 4 March 2027. It is not a long-term status, so the firm also advises Ukrainian clients on moving to employment, business or family-based permits.

Israeli clients usually arrive through a business — a BFU-registered company, permits for key staff, family reunification. Savvas Kalopetrides is a member of the Israel-Cyprus Chamber of Commerce.

- **May 2023** — fourth revision of the investor Immigration Permit policy: €300,000 across four categories, €50,000 income, three-yearly criminal record checks.
- **June 2023** — revised Strategy for Attracting Businesses and Talent: €2,500 minimum salary for highly skilled staff of foreign-interest companies.
- **December 2023** — Law 149(I)/2023 reforms naturalisation: 12 months plus seven of ten years, Greek B1, fast track for highly qualified employees.
- **2024–2025** — Digital Nomad Visa quota reached, scheme suspended and later reopened.
- **June 2025** — EU temporary protection for Ukrainians extended to 4 March 2027.
