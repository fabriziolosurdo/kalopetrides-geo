## How it works in Cyprus

**The courts.** Civil and commercial claims are started in the District Court of the district with jurisdiction; there is one District Court for each of the island's districts, including Larnaca. Appeals in civil cases are heard by the appellate court, with the Supreme Court as the final court in limited cases. Specialised courts exist for certain commercial and admiralty disputes; which court is competent, and whether it is operating for the type of claim, is checked at the time a claim is brought.

**The Civil Procedure Rules.** The Supreme Court issued new Civil Procedure Rules in 2023, which have since been amended by further procedural rules. Which set of rules governs a case depends on when it was filed, so both the new and the previous rules remain relevant for a time. Under either set, the parties are expected to prepare their case before filing, to disclose their position early, and to consider settlement at each stage; failing to do so can affect the costs the court awards.

**Limitation.** The Limitation of Actions Law of 2012, Law 66(I)/2012, sets a period of six years for claims in contract (section 7) and six years for civil wrongs (section 6(1)), with three years for negligence, nuisance and breach of statutory duty (section 6(2)). Section 4 sets a general ten-year limit unless another law provides otherwise, and section 22 allows the court to extend a period by up to two years where it considers this fair and reasonable. The Law also contains transitional and suspension provisions, so the period should always be checked for the specific claim.

**Enforcement.** A Cyprus judgment is enforced through the court's enforcement procedures against the debtor's assets. Foreign judgments are enforced in Cyprus under the EU rules on recognition and enforcement for judgments from other Member States, under bilateral treaties or Cyprus legislation for certain other countries, and otherwise by an action on the judgment.

## For clients abroad

A large part of our litigation work is for clients who are not in Cyprus: a foreign buyer in dispute with a developer over delivery or title, a foreign company owed money by a Cyprus counterparty, a shareholder in a Cyprus company in conflict with the other shareholders, an insurer or insured in a claim. A claimant does not need to live in Cyprus.
