  - q: What changed with the new Civil Procedure Rules?
    a: The new Civil Procedure Rules entered into force on 1 September 2023 and apply to civil cases filed after that date; cases filed earlier continue under the old rules. The periods run from when the cause of action is complete. Because the rules are detailed and exceptions exist, we check the applicable period for your claim at the first meeting.

    a: Yes. A claimant does not need to be resident in Cyprus. ## How it works in Cyprus

**The courts.** Civil and commercial claims are started in the District Court of the district with jurisdiction; there is one District Court for each of the island's districts, including Larnaca. Appeals in civil cases are heard by the Court of Appeal, which began hearing appeals in July 2023 following a constitutional amendment, with the Supreme Court as the final court of appeal in limited cases. A Commercial Court and an Admiralty Court were established by legislation in 2022 for commercial disputes above a high monetary threshold; their operational status should be checked at the time a claim is brought.

**The Civil Procedure Rules.** New Civil Procedure Rules entered into force on 1 September 2023 and apply to civil cases filed after that date. Cases filed before that date continue under the previous rules, which were modelled on the English rules of 1958, so both sets of rules remain relevant for a time. The new rules state an overriding objective of enabling the court to deal with cases justly and at proportionate cost, and require the parties to help the court pursue that objective. They give judges active case-management powers, introduce pre-action protocols that parties are expected to follow before filing, and provide for settlement offers that protect the offering party on costs if the other side refuses and later recovers less. Failure to follow a protocol can be taken into account in costs and other orders.

**Limitation.** The Limitation of Actions Law of 2012, Law 66(I)/2012, sets a general period of six years for claims in contract and six years for civil wrongs, with three years for certain claims such as negligence, nuisance and breach of statutory duty, within a general ten-year bar; the court may in some cases extend a period by up to two years. The periods are counted in accordance with the Law, which contains transitional and suspension provisions, and should always be checked for the specific claim.

**Enforcement.** A Cyprus judgment is enforced through the court's enforcement procedures against the debtor's assets. Foreign judgments are enforced in Cyprus under the EU rules on recognition and enforcement for judgments from other Member States, under bilateral treaties or Cyprus legislation for certain other countries, and otherwise by an action on the judgment.

## For clients abroad

A large part of our litigation work is for clients who are not in Cyprus: a foreign buyer in dispute with a developer over delivery or title, a foreign company owed money by a Cyprus counterparty, a shareholder in a Cyprus company in conflict with the other shareholders, an insurer or insured in a claim. A claimant does not need to live in Cyprus. We take instructions by email and video call, keep you informed of each step and each hearing, and arrange your attendance only where your evidence is needed.
