  - q: Can a foreigner buy property in Cyprus?
    a: >-
      Yes. Citizens of EU and EEA states, and companies incorporated in the EU or
      EEA, are not treated as "aliens" under the Immovable Property Acquisition
      (Aliens) Law, Cap. 109, and buy on the same footing as Cypriots. Nationals of
      other countries (for example the UK, Russia, Ukraine, Israel, Lebanon,
      Switzerland) can also buy, but they need the prior consent of the Council of

      application goes to the District Officer of the district where the property
      lies. A contract can be signed and deposited before the permit is issued; the
      permit is needed for the final transfer.
  - q: What is the Council of Ministers permit and how do I apply?
    a: >-
      Under section 3 of Cap. 109, a non-EU/EEA national needs the prior consent of
      the Council of Ministers to acquire immovable property in Cyprus, other than
      by inheritance. The application is made on Form Comm. 145 to the District
      Officer and asks for personal details, financial standing (with a bank
      statement), any other property held in Cyprus, details of the property and
      its owner, the price and the intended use. Supporting documents include the
      title deed, the survey plan, the contract of sale and passport copies. The
      firm prepares and files the application and follows it up until the consent
      is issued.
  - q: Why should the contract of sale be deposited with the Land Registry?
    a: >-
      Depositing the contract at the District Lands Office brings it under the Sale
      of Immovable Property (Specific Performance) Law 81(I)/2011. The deposited
      contract is recorded against the property, so the seller cannot transfer it
      to someone else free of your rights, and you can ask the court for an order
      of specific performance if the seller fails to transfer. The contract must be
      deposited within six months of signing, and the Land Registry charges a fee
      of €50.00 for the deposit. For contracts signed after 12 December 2023, a
      Land Registry search certificate must be attached.

    a: >-
      VAT at the standard rate of 19% applies to the sale of new buildings (and the
      land they stand on) sold before first occupation, where the planning
      application was filed after 1 May 2004. Resale property is generally outside
      VAT; transfer fees apply instead. A reduced rate of 5% is available for a
      dwelling used as the buyer's permanent primary residence, subject to the area

## How it works in Cyprus

Cyprus has a centralised Land Registry, the Department of Lands and Surveys, which keeps a register of every parcel of land and every separately titled building or unit. Ownership passes only when the transfer is registered at the District Lands Office. Signing a contract and paying the price do not, by themselves, make you the owner.

### 1. Due diligence on the title

Before any money changes hands, the firm obtains a search certificate from the Land Registry for the property and its registered owner. It shows the registration details and any encumbrances and prohibitions recorded against the property: mortgages, memos from court judgments, deposited contracts of sale, prohibitions on transfer. The Department issues it at a published fee of €10.00 (simple) or €15.00 (with encumbrances and prohibitions); an applicant who is not the owner declares an interest under the Immovable Property Law, Cap. 224.

The firm also checks the type of property. A house or apartment with its own separate title deed is the simplest case. Land, plots under division and units in a building without final approval and separate titles each raise their own questions: planning and building permits, the state of the division, and whether the developer's bank has a mortgage over the whole development. For land, the firm checks the planning zone, building coefficient, access and Town Planning conditions.

### 2. The contract of sale

The contract must be in writing, identify the parties and the property, state the price and be signed by everyone. For foreign clients it is usually drafted in English, with a deposit at signing and the balance on transfer or against building stages. The firm negotiates the clauses that matter in practice: the completion date and the consequences of delay, who pays which fees, what happens if the Council of Ministers consent is refused, the release of any seller's mortgage, and, in off-plan purchases, the specification, stage payments and penalties.

### 3. Deposit with the Land Registry (specific performance)

The single most important protection for a buyer in Cyprus is to deposit the signed contract at the District Lands Office under the Sale of Immovable Property (Specific Performance) Law 81(I)/2011. Once deposited, the contract is recorded against the property. The seller cannot transfer or charge the property free of your rights, and if the seller fails to transfer when the time comes you can apply to the court for an order of specific performance, so that the property passes to you by court order rather than by the seller's goodwill.

The Department of Lands and Surveys publishes the conditions. The property must be registered in the seller's name; the contract must be deposited within six months of signing, unless the court allows a late deposit; the application is made on Form Δ.Ε.314 with the original contract, the survey plan and, for a unit, the floor plans, all signed by the parties; the fee is €50.00. For contracts signed after 12 December 2023, a Land Registry search certificate dated within five working days of the contract must be attached.

Law 132(I)/2023, in force since 12 December 2023, addresses the problem that caused most buyer losses in the past: property sold while the seller's bank still held a mortgage over it. If the seller's bank has a mortgage on the property, you choose at deposit between two forms. Where a mortgage is registered, the deposit is accompanied either by Form A, signed by the mortgagee, seller and buyer and stating the amount that must be paid to release the property, or by Form C, in which the buyer confirms that they proceed without that mechanism. Once the buyer pays the amount stated in Form A, the mortgagee must release the property; if it refuses, the Director of Lands and Surveys has powers to transfer the property anyway, and the buyer can seek a specific performance order. The firm advises which route is right in each case and does not let a client sign Form C without understanding what it gives up.

### 4. Transfer and registration

On the completion date the parties, or their attorneys under a power of attorney, attend the District Lands Office. The seller produces the tax clearance (Form N313) and the receipts showing that municipal or community taxes, sewerage fees and water charges are paid. The buyer pays the transfer fees. The Land Registry registers the transfer and issues the new title deed in the buyer's name. Where a buyer cannot travel, the firm acts under a power of attorney.

## Buying property in Cyprus as a foreigner

### EU and EEA citizens

The Immovable Property Acquisition (Aliens) Law, Cap. 109, defines an "alien" as a person who is not a citizen of the Republic, but expressly excludes citizens of EU member states and of EEA states, and legal persons incorporated under the law of an EU or EEA state with their seat, central administration or principal place of business there. A German, Dutch, Polish or Austrian buyer therefore buys in Cyprus on the same terms as a Cypriot: no permit, no limit on the number or size of properties, the same fees.

### Non-EU buyers and the Council of Ministers permit

For everyone else, section 3 of Cap. 109 requires the prior consent of the Council of Ministers before immovable property can be acquired, other than by inheritance. The law treats as an "acquisition" not only a purchase but also a lease exceeding 33 years, the purchase of shares that would bring a Cyprus company holding property under alien control, and the creation of a trust for the benefit of an alien. A registration made in breach of section 3 is void, and a contract that would give an alien rights to property confers none until the consent is obtained. Where the area acquired exceeds what is strictly necessary for a residence or professional premises, additional conditions set by regulations apply.

In practice this does not prevent non-EU buyers from purchasing. The contract is signed and deposited at the Land Registry in the usual way, and the application for consent runs in parallel. It is made on Form Comm. 145 to the District Officer of the district where the property lies. The form asks for the applicant's personal details, nationality and passport, financial position (capital, income, property abroad, with a bank statement attached), any other property already held in Cyprus by the applicant or family members, details of the property and its current owner, the price, the intended use and residence plans. The listed supporting documents include the title deed, the survey plan, the building or division permit where relevant, the contract of sale, the floor plan, proof of financial standing and passport copies for the applicant and spouse. Where the buyer is a company, the corporate documents and details of shareholders and directors are added.

The firm prepares the application, assembles the documents, files it and follows it up with the District Administration. The form itself reminds applicants that the Government takes no responsibility for the contract between buyer and seller; the firm's role is to make sure the contract and the deposit protect the buyer while the consent is pending.

### Compliance checks

Cyprus is an EU member state and EU restrictive measures apply in full. Banks, the Land Registry and lawyers are bound by anti-money-laundering rules and will ask for source-of-funds evidence. The firm tells clients at the outset what will be required.

## Selling property in Cyprus

A seller needs the title deed, a tax clearance certificate from the Tax Department (Form N313), proof that local taxes and utility charges are paid and, if the property is mortgaged, the bank's release. The firm prepares the contract, handles the deposit, deals with the buyer's lawyer and the bank, and attends the transfer.

Capital gains tax applies at 20% on the gain from the disposal of immovable property situated in Cyprus. The same tax reaches shares in companies that hold Cyprus property directly or indirectly, where at least 20% of the value of the shares derives from such property. From 1 January 2026, under Law 242(I)/2025, an individual's lifetime exemptions are €150,000 for a principal residence occupied for at least five years on a plot of up to 1.5 dekaria, €50,000 for agricultural land disposed of by a person whose main occupation is farming, and €30,000 for any other disposal; a person may claim one of these, whichever is larger. Acquisition cost, inflation adjustment and allowable expenses reduce the taxable gain, and the firm computes the position before the price is agreed. A separate levy on sales of immovable property, and on certain transfers of shares in property-holding companies, is payable to the Central Agency for Equal Distribution of Burdens at the rate in force.

## Leases and lease registration

The firm drafts and reviews residential and commercial leases for landlords and tenants, including rent review, break and renewal clauses, deposits, repairs and assignment. A lease for a period exceeding 15 years can be registered at the District Lands Office on Form N.304, with the original contract bearing certified signatures, the landlord's tax clearance and the local-tax receipts. Registration gives the tenant a leasehold that can be transferred, mortgaged or passed to heirs. The published fee is a percentage of the market value of the leasehold with a minimum of €100.00. Non-EU tenants should remember that a lease exceeding 33 years is an acquisition under Cap. 109 and needs the Council of Ministers consent.

## Developers, construction and disputes

Off-plan purchases and construction projects produce many of the real estate disputes in Cyprus: late delivery, defects, deviations from the agreed specification, developments with a bank mortgage over the whole site, and title deeds issued years after completion. The firm acts for buyers against developers and for developers and contractors in their own disputes, through negotiation, mediation, arbitration where the contract provides for it, and litigation in the Cyprus courts. Where a deposited contract exists, specific performance is usually the first line of protection; where it does not, the firm looks at damages, cancellation and the recovery of sums paid.

On the development side, the firm advises on land assembly, joint ventures between landowners and developers (including exchange-of-land arrangements), construction contracts, planning conditions and the division of completed buildings into separate titles.
