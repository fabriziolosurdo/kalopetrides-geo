If you live in Cyprus or own property or a company here, your estate will one day have to be administered under Cyprus procedure, and possibly under Cyprus law. Kalopetrides Law LLC drafts wills that work in Cyprus, advises on which country's law should govern your succession, and handles probate and the administration of estates before the Cyprus courts, including estates with assets in several countries.

## How it works in Cyprus

**Which law applies.** Cyprus is bound by Regulation (EU) No 650/2012, the EU Succession Regulation, which applies to the successions of persons who die on or after 17 August 2015 in all EU Member States except Denmark and Ireland. Under the Regulation, the general rule is that the law of the state in which the deceased had his or her habitual residence at the time of death governs the succession as a whole. A person may instead choose, in a will, the law of a state whose nationality he or she possesses. For a German, Austrian, Dutch or Polish national living in Cyprus, this choice decides whether Cyprus law or the law of the home country applies to the whole estate, and it should be made deliberately.

**Forced heirship under Cyprus law.** Where Cyprus law governs, the Wills and Succession Law, Cap. 195, limits testamentary freedom. Section 41 provides that, where the deceased leaves a spouse and a child or descendant of a child, the disposable portion of the estate may not exceed one quarter of its net value; where the deceased leaves a spouse or a parent but no child or descendant, the disposable portion may not exceed one half; where none of these survive, the whole estate is disposable. The remainder passes to the statutory heirs regardless of the will.

**Formal validity of a will.** Section 23 of Cap. 195 requires a will to be in writing, signed by the testator at the foot or end of the document, with the signature made or acknowledged before two or more witnesses present at the same time, who attest and sign in the presence of the testator and of each other. Each sheet of a multi-page will must be signed or initialled.

**Probate and administration.** After a death, the estate cannot be dealt with until the court has issued a grant: probate to the executor named in a valid will, or letters of administration where there is no will or no executor able to act. The procedure is governed by the Administration of Estates Law, Cap. 189, and grants are issued through the probate registry of the District Court. The executor or administrator collects the assets, settles debts and taxes, and distributes the estate to the beneficiaries.

**Cross-border estates.** Where assets lie in more than one EU country, a European Certificate of Succession issued under the Regulation allows heirs, executors and administrators to prove their status in the other participating Member States without a separate procedure. Foreign wills and foreign grants can also be recognised in Cyprus, but the route depends on the country concerned and on the documents available.

## For foreign residents and owners of Cyprus property

Most of the people who ask us about wills are foreign nationals who have bought a home in the Larnaca district or hold shares in a Cyprus company. The questions that matter in practice are:

- whether a will made at home covers the Cyprus assets, and whether a separate Cyprus will would make the administration faster and cheaper for the heirs;
- whether to choose the law of your nationality in the will, and what that means for your spouse and children under that law compared with the Cyprus rules;
- how a Cyprus will and a home-country will can coexist without one revoking the other;
- who will act as executor in Cyprus and how the heirs abroad will receive their shares.

We answer these questions for your specific situation before any document is drafted.
