Cyprus is a practical place for a foreign couple to marry: the civil marriage is performed by the municipality, there is no residence requirement, the documents are straightforward if prepared in advance, and the certificate is recognised abroad once legalised. Kalopetrides Law LLC assists local and foreign couples with the notice of marriage, the documents and the timing, and advises on the recognition of the marriage at home and on its consequences under Cyprus law.

## How it works in Cyprus

**The law.** Civil marriages in Cyprus are governed by the Marriage Law of 2003, Law 104(I)/2003. Under section 3, marriages are solemnised by a Marriage Officer: the Mayor, the Vice-Mayor or a member of the municipal council authorised by the Mayor, or a person designated by the Minister of Interior. In practice the couple deals with the civil marriage office of the municipality where they wish to marry, for example the Municipality of Larnaca.

**Notice of marriage.** Under sections 4 and 5, the parties sign a notice of marriage and present it in person to the Marriage Officer of the municipality of their choice. Before fixing the date, under section 8, each party makes a solemn affirmation and confirms in writing that they are not aware of any impediment or legal obstacle to the marriage and that any required consents have been obtained or are not needed. The Marriage Officer may ask for supporting certificates and declarations, and will not issue the certificate of notice if the requirements of the Law are not met.

**Timing.** Under section 6, the date and place of the marriage are fixed in the certificate of notice. The date may not be earlier than fifteen days, and not later than three months, from the day the notice was delivered. Under section 7, the Marriage Officer may fix an earlier date, within the fifteen days, where considered appropriate, on payment of an increased fee. Municipalities describe the two routes as the ordinary notice of marriage and the notice of special procedure. A couple using the ordinary notice should plan to be in Cyprus for about three weeks; with the special procedure the marriage can take place within a few days of the notice.

**Documents.** Municipalities ask each party for a valid passport or identity document, an official document from the home country proving that the person is free to marry (certificate of single status, certificate of no impediment or equivalent), a final divorce decree for a divorced party or the death certificate of a former spouse for a widowed party, and written parental consent where a party is under eighteen. Documents must be in Greek or English, or translated into one of them, and legalised by the competent authority or embassy, usually with an apostille. The exact requirements depend on the country that issues the document.

**Fees.** Each municipality publishes its own fees for the notice of marriage, the special procedure, copies of the certificate and ceremonies outside working hours, and they should be confirmed with the municipality at the time of booking.

**After the ceremony.** The municipality issues the marriage certificate. For use abroad, the certificate is legalised with an apostille and, where required, translated. We arrange both.

## For foreign couples

Foreign couples ask us the same practical questions: which document from home proves that we are free to marry, does it need an apostille, can we marry on a specific date, and what does our home registry need afterwards. The answers depend on the country of origin and sometimes on the municipality. We check them for your case before you travel, so that the notice is accepted at the first visit.
