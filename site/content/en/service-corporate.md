Cyprus company law is English-style common law, written in the Companies Law, Cap. 113, and the Registrar of Companies files in Greek. ## How it works in Cyprus

A Cyprus private company limited by shares ("Ltd") is the vehicle almost every foreign client uses. Under the Companies Law it must restrict the transfer of its shares, may have between one and fifty members, and may not offer shares to the public. The nominal capital and its division into shares are stated in the memorandum.

### Step 1 — Name approval

The proposed name is submitted to the Registrar of Companies, which checks it against existing entities and trade marks and rejects names that are similar, misleading or undesirable. The fee is €10 per proposed name, plus €20 for the accelerated procedure. Once approved, the name is reserved and the incorporation application must be filed within six months. Restricted words need the consent of the competent authority.

### Step 2 — Incorporation filing

The application is filed through the Registrar's e-filing system or by hand. It consists of three short forms plus the memorandum and articles of association:

- Form HE1, a statutory declaration sworn before the court by the lawyer entrusted with the incorporation. Only a lawyer can make this declaration.
- The memorandum and articles of association, signed in Greek. A certified translation can be filed alongside for an official file of translations.
- Form HE2, notifying the address of the registered office, which must be in Cyprus.
- Form HE3, notifying the first directors and secretary, with identity details, date of birth, profession and full address for each individual.

The filing fee is €165 (€235 for a company without share capital), plus €100 for the accelerated procedure and €160 for a file of translations. The Registrar's published review criteria are strict on formalities: handwritten share numbers in the memorandum, numbered pages, matching names across all documents, dates in the right order. Most rejections come from these details.

### Step 3 — Certificate of incorporation

When the Registrar is satisfied, it issues the certificate of incorporation, which marks the start of the company's legal existence. Optionally, for €120 (or €130 without a certified copy of the memorandum and articles) the Registrar issues the set of certificates that banks and foreign counterparties ask for: shareholders, directors and secretary, registered office, and certified copies of the memorandum and articles and of the certificate itself.

### Step 4 — Directors, secretary and registered office

The Companies Law requires every private company to have at least one director and a secretary. In a single-member private company limited by shares the sole director may also be the secretary. From the date of the certificate the company must maintain a registered office in Cyprus where notices can be served; changes of address are filed within fourteen days.

Where directors live and where the board meets are not conditions of registration, but they decide how banks and foreign tax authorities treat the company. The firm discusses this before the forms are signed.

### Step 5 — Registrations after incorporation

The Registrar's guidance lists what follows the certificate:

- Registration with the Tax Department and a tax identification number within sixty days of incorporation.
- VAT registration once taxable supplies reach the thresholds in force, and registration as an employer with Social Insurance Services before hiring.
- Entry of the beneficial owners in the Register of Beneficial Owners within ninety days of incorporation.
- An annual return filed with the Registrar each calendar year with a copy of the financial statements, and audited financial statements with the tax return to the Tax Department.

The annual company levy that older guides still mention was abolished from 2024 by the Companies (Amendment) Law 25(I)/2024, as announced by the Registrar.

## The beneficial owners register

Every company incorporated under Cap. 113 must record in the Register of Beneficial Owners the natural persons who ultimately own or control it. New companies file within ninety days of incorporation, changes within forty-five days, and every company must confirm its data between 1 October and 31 December each year, even if nothing has changed. The Registrar's notice for the 2025 confirmation cycle cited fines of €100 for the first day of default plus €50 for each further day, up to €5,000.

A Cyprus company therefore does not offer anonymity. It offers an EU company with a public register, which is what counterparties, banks and tax authorities in Germany, the Netherlands or Israel now expect. The firm files and confirms the register for its clients.

## Opening a bank account: what actually happens

Incorporation is a filing exercise; opening the bank account is a risk assessment by a third party. Cyprus credit institutions apply the Central Bank of Cyprus Prevention of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Directive of 2025, published in the Official Gazette on 2 May 2025, together with their internal policies. A bank will ask for:

- Identification of every director, shareholder and beneficial owner: certified passport copies and proof of residential address.
- The corporate certificates, the memorandum and articles, and the UBO register extract.
- A description of the business, the expected counterparties and countries, and the anticipated turnover.
- Evidence of the source of funds for the initial deposit and of the beneficial owner's source of wealth.

The 2025 Directive moved the banks to a risk-based approach and allows certified copies in place of originals, which has made the process more predictable for well-documented clients. It has not made it faster for shell structures, complex ownership chains or high-risk jurisdictions, which attract enhanced due diligence. Clients from Russian-speaking countries should expect additional questions on the ownership chain and sanctions exposure, and should raise these with Savvas Kalopetrides before incorporation.

## Tax position of a Cyprus company after the 2025 reform

The tax reform laws published in the Official Gazette on 31 December 2025 changed the picture for anyone setting up in 2026:

- **Corporate tax at 15%.** The Second Schedule of the Income Tax Law now provides that companies are subject to corporate tax at 15% on every euro of taxable income, replacing the previous 12.5%.
- **Tax residency by incorporation.** Section 2 of the Income Tax Law treats a company as resident in Cyprus if its control and management are exercised here, or if it is incorporated in Cyprus under the Companies Law, unless a double tax treaty provides otherwise. A company that moves its registered office to Cyprus is treated as incorporated here. For a founder living in Germany or the Netherlands, the home country may still claim residency under its own rules, and the treaty tie-breaker decides.
- **Stamp duty repealed.** Law 239(I)/2025 repealed the Stamp Duty Laws with effect from 1 January 2026, so share transfer instruments, loan agreements and commercial contracts no longer need to be stamped.
- **Deemed distribution abolished.** Under the Special Contribution for the Defence amendments, the deemed dividend distribution rules no longer apply to profits generated after 31 December 2025.

Personal income tax and the treatment of individual shareholders are covered on the firm's tax page. The outcome depends on where the founder lives, where the work is done and which treaty applies.

## Substance: what Cyprus asks and what your home country asks

Cyprus company law does not prescribe a minimum level of office space, staff or local directors. What matters is the tax residency test above, the expectations of the bank, and the anti-abuse rules of the country where the founder lives: a German or Austrian tax office looks at where management decisions are really taken, a Dutch or Polish one applies its own controlled foreign company rules, Israeli residents have their own reporting duties. The firm builds the structure to match the client's real activity in Cyprus and documents it: board minutes held in Cyprus, a real registered office, local service providers, accounting kept here.

## Shareholder agreements and governance

The memorandum and articles are public documents filed in Greek on a standard model. The real deal between partners belongs in a shareholders' agreement governed by Cyprus law and drafted in English, which stays private. The firm drafts and negotiates:

- Reserved matters and board composition, so that a minority investor or a foreign co-founder knows which decisions need their consent.
- Pre-emption, tag-along and drag-along provisions, and the valuation mechanism for a departing partner, aligned with the articles.
- Vesting and leaver provisions for founder shares, and option arrangements for key staff.
- Deadlock and dispute resolution clauses, including arbitration seated in Cyprus or abroad.

The firm amends the articles at the same time so that they stay consistent with the agreement, and files the change with the Registrar.

## Share transfers, M&A and restructuring

A transfer of shares in a Cyprus private company is approved by the board under the articles, entered in the register of members at the registered office, and notified to the Registrar within fourteen days on the prescribed form, with a filing fee of €20 (plus €20 accelerated). Late notification attracts late filing charges. The firm handles the due diligence on the target, the share purchase agreement with warranties and indemnities, completion and the post-completion filings, including the UBO update within forty-five days.

For larger transactions the firm advises on acquisitions and disposals of Cyprus companies and holding companies; joint ventures between a foreign investor and a Cyprus partner; group reorganisations, share-for-share exchanges and transfers of a business as a going concern; redomiciliation of a foreign company into Cyprus, a Registrar procedure in which a temporary certificate of continuation is followed by the final certificate; and cross-border mergers where the resulting company is a Cyprus company. The Income Tax Law contains a reorganisation regime whose conditions were adjusted again in 2025; the firm checks them for each transaction rather than assuming neutrality.
